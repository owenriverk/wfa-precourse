Scale it to the trip

The checklist above is a solid day-trip baseline for two people. Scale by time-to-help, not just days out.

TripAdjust the baseline
Short day hike, front-countryTrim to gloves, bleeding control, blister kit, meds, whistle, phone.
Full day, remoteThe full baseline above. Add the satellite messenger if you're out of coverage.
Multi-day backcountryDouble consumables (gauze, meds, gloves), add a low-reading thermometer, wound-care refills, and a real repair kit.
Group or trip leaderScale consumables by head count, collect everyone's medical info and prescriptions location before leaving, and carry a group shelter.

By activity

Match the kit to the way your sport actually hurts people.

🏍 Motorcycle / dual-sport

High-energy trauma: tourniquet and shears earn their weight (cutting riding gear off beats wrestling it off). Review helmet removal — Lesson 7 before you need it. Strap the kit to the bike you'd still have after a crash.

🛶 Rivers & open water

Everything in dry bags, twice. Add a full hypothermia setup — dry insulation layer, blanket/bivy, sugar-rich food. Review drowning and cold-water immersion — Lesson 14.

🥾 Desert & heat

Extra water beats extra gauze: add oral rehydration salts, electrolytes, and something to make shade. Review heat illness — Lesson 8 — cool first, transport second.

❄️ Winter & altitude

Chemical heat packs, foam pad to insulate from the ground, low-reading thermometer, and calories. Review hypothermia and frostbite — Lesson 9.

🎒

Skills over stuff. A tourniquet you've never practiced with, or a splint you've never molded, is dead weight. Buy less, practice more — and restock the kit after every trip, because the gauze you used last time is the gauze you won't have next time. Commercial pre-built kits are a fine starting skeleton; upgrade the gloves, add the tourniquet and irrigation syringe, and make it yours. Not sure where a skill is taught?
