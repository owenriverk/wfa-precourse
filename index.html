How it works

Work through the lessons in order — each one builds on the last. Expect 20 to 45 minutes per lesson. The full course syllabus lists every lesson and the skills it teaches.

1

Read a lesson

Short sections with the key decisions, warning signs, and step-by-step care for each problem.

2

Work the scenarios

Realistic cases with hidden discussions so you can commit to a plan before you see the reasoning.

3

Check yourself

Every lesson ends with a quiz that explains each answer. Retake it as often as you like.

4

Take the final exam

40 questions across lessons 1–14. Pass, and print a record of completion with your name, score, and date.

Questions

The short version: it's free, it's open, and it's not a certification.

Is this really free?

Yes. There is no account required, no paywall, and no tracking. Your progress and quiz scores are stored in your own browser by default. If you want them to follow you between devices, an optional email sign-in can back them up and sync them — off unless you turn it on, deletable any time. The whole site is open source on GitHub.

Who is this for?

Anyone who spends time where an ambulance can't reach you quickly: hikers, backpackers, paddlers, dual-sport and adventure riders, climbers, hunters, trip leaders, and parents taking kids outdoors. No medical background is needed. It also works well as preparation for, or a refresher after, an in-person wilderness first aid course.

Does finishing the course make me certified?

No. Completing the final exam produces a record of completion — a personal study record showing your name, score, and date. Wilderness first aid skills like splinting, wound care, and CPR need hands-on practice with an instructor. Use this course to build the knowledge so that in-person training can focus on the hands-on part.

What level is the material?

Lay-rescuer wilderness first aid. Nothing here requires equipment or authority you won't have: the focus is on recognizing what's wrong, doing what a first aider can safely do, and deciding how urgently to get help. Helping a patient use their own epinephrine auto-injector or inhaler, and common over-the-counter medications, are within scope. The lesson-by-lesson curriculum spells out what each one covers before you start.

Can I use this during an emergency?

Please don't rely on it for that. Call your local emergency number, or activate your satellite messenger or beacon. The mnemonics above and each lesson's Summary section are designed to be skimmable, but the course is for learning ahead of time, not for looking things up while someone is hurt.

I found a mistake. Can I contribute?

Yes, and thank you. Open an issue or pull request on GitHub. Corrections that cite current first aid guidance are especially welcome. Instructors are welcome to use the course as pre-work for their own classes.

⚠️

Educational use only. This course supports wilderness first aid training and does not replace hands-on instruction or professional medical care. Content follows current wilderness medicine best practices and evidence-based first aid guidance, but guidance changes — when in doubt, get help.
