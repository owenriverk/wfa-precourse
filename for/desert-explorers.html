Heat is the terrain. Water is the clock.

Canyon country strips the margin out of ordinary mistakes: the heat spectrum runs its course fast, water is arithmetic, and the road out depends on a machine. This course drills the heat doctrine hard, plus the scene judgment for the day the drainage upstream turns brown.

Your version of a bad day

  • She stopped sweating an hour ago. Mile nine, no shade taller than a knee, and her answers stop matching your questions. Heat stroke gets cooled now, here, with the water you were rationing — not at the trailhead.
  • Blue sky, brown roar. The storm is forty miles upstream and the slot narrows ahead. Reading the drainage — not the sky over your head — is the scene call that keeps one patient from becoming five.
  • The truck is the casualty. A sheared valve stem two hours down a washboard road, one gallon left per person. Stay-or-walk is an evacuation problem, and the arithmetic is unforgiving in July.

Then pressure-test it

Interactive scenarios — one decision at a time, tempting mistakes included, debrief at the end:

  • Cool First — cool first, with the water you were saving
  • Sharpie on the Leg — the snakebite, done by doctrine
  • Two Go, One Stays — the messenger plan when driving out isn’t an option

The certification question, honestly. “Wilderness First Aid” isn’t a regulated credential. No government body defines what a WFA card means — it means whatever the company that printed it says it means. What counts in front of a patient is whether you can do the work. This course teaches the work, free, and issues a record of completion — not a certificate, and we won’t pretend otherwise. Slot-canyon rescue is rope work, and rope work belongs to a rope course — we don’t teach it. If an outfitter or guide program requires a provider’s card, take their class; you’ll arrive already fluent in the heat doctrine you’ll use most. If nobody requires you to hold a card, what you need are the skills, not the laminate.

What this is

Fifteen lessons, a 40-question knowledge check, sixteen practice scenarios, a printable field reference card, and a kit checklist. Free, no account, nothing recorded about you, source on GitHub. It teaches decision-making, not hands-on skill — practice the hands-on parts on a real, padded, complaining friend.
